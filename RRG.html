How to read a Relative Rotation Graph

The one-sentence version

An RRG answers two questions at once: is this sector beating the benchmark, and is that lead growing or shrinking. Everything on the chart is relative to NIFTY 50 — nothing here tells you whether the market itself is going up.

The two axes

RS-Ratio (horizontal) — relative strength. Right of centre means the sector has been outperforming the benchmark; left means underperforming.

RS-Momentum (vertical) — the rate of change of that relative strength. Above centre means the sector's relative performance is improving; below means it is deteriorating.

Both are normalised around 100, so the crosshair in the middle is the benchmark. A sector sitting exactly at the centre is moving in line with NIFTY 50.

The four quadrants

Improving
Still weaker than the benchmark, but catching up. Early recovery — often where the next leaders come from.
ratio < 100 · momentum > 100
Leading
Stronger than the benchmark and still pulling away. Established leadership.
ratio > 100 · momentum > 100
Lagging
Weaker than the benchmark and still falling behind. Sustained underperformance.
ratio < 100 · momentum < 100
Weakening
Still ahead of the benchmark, but the lead is shrinking. Leadership may be rolling over.
ratio > 100 · momentum < 100

The quadrants are laid out so that the strong side is on the right and the improving side is on top — which is why Improving sits top-left and Weakening bottom-right.

Rotation is clockwise

The idealised cycle runs Improving → Leading → Weakening → Lagging → Improving, clockwise around the centre. Momentum turns before strength does, so a sector normally enters Weakening before it ever reaches Lagging.

It is an idealisation, not a rule. Sectors routinely cut across the middle, reverse, or sit in one quadrant for months.

Read the tail, not just the dot

The dot is only where a sector is now. The tail behind it shows how it got there, and that is usually the more useful half:

  • Long tail — moving fast and decisively.
  • Short, knotted tail — going nowhere; the signal is weak.
  • Leading but curling down — heading for Weakening. Be careful adding fresh exposure.
  • Lagging but curling up — possible recovery, not yet confirmed.
  • Improving and pushing up-and-right — the most interesting quadrant: early strength.

The Heading column is the compass bearing of the last leg, so NE means up-and-right (strengthening on both axes) and SW means down-and-left.

Sectors, then stocks

Use the dropdown to switch from the eleven sector indices to the constituents of any one sector. For stocks you can pick the benchmark:

  • vs NIFTY 50 — how the stock is doing against the whole market.
  • vs Sector — how it is doing against its own peers, which is the better question for picking leaders within a sector.

Weekly vs Daily

Weekly is the standard and the default: slower, cleaner, better for medium-term rotation. Daily reacts sooner but is noisier, so it uses heavier smoothing here (n=20 against n=10 weekly). Values from the two are not comparable — each is its own calculation.

How these numbers are calculated

RS          = 100 × Sector / Benchmark
RS-Ratio    = 100 × EMA(RS, n) / EMA(EMA(RS, n), n)
RS-Momentum = 100 × RS-Ratio / EMA(RS-Ratio, n)

This is the widely used approximation of the original JdK RS-Ratio / RS-Momentum, not the proprietary formula used by commercial RRG platforms. Quadrants and direction are faithful; the absolute distance from 100 will not match a vendor's chart, so do not compare the raw numbers across tools.

One consequence of this formula: RS-Momentum varies over a much narrower band than RS-Ratio. The chart therefore scales each axis independently around 100. The centre lines still sit at exactly 100, so quadrant membership is unaffected.

Where the prices come from

Two years of hourly bars, resampled to daily closes and then to weekly closes — the same treatment for every series including the benchmark. Yahoo carries daily bars for only three of the eleven sector indices, so resampling is the only way to give all eleven an identical basis. Checked against real daily bars where both exist: median deviation 0.05%.

Recently listed names

The smoothing needs history to settle. A name listed within the last few months is marked short in the table: its dot is real, but it will shift more than an established name's as more data arrives. Measured on this data, 105 weeks of history gives a position stable to 0.000 when a quarter of the series is removed; 30 weeks shifts by about 0.1, which is a couple of percent of the axis.

What it will not tell you

  • Nothing about absolute direction. A sector can sit in Leading while falling, if it is falling more slowly than the market.
  • Nothing about valuation, breadth or liquidity.
  • Not a signal on its own. Pair it with price trend and the behaviour of the sector's own constituents.
